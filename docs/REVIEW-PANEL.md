# Review panel

Six reviewers with different backgrounds reviewed the site in three rounds. After each round the feedback was applied and the same reviewers re-checked the result.

## Scores out of 10

| Reviewer | Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 | Final verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Senior visual designer / accessibility specialist | 7.5 | 8.5 | 9 | Ready to launch |
| Technical SEO consultant | 7 | 8.5 | 9 | Approved for launch |
| Regional property manager (1,200 units) | 6.5 | 8 | 8.5 | Ship it; would send a pilot scope |
| B2B conversion strategist / copywriter | 6.5 | 8 | 8.5 | Approve |
| Short-term rental operator (35 listings) | 6 | 7.5 | 8 | Ship it; would request a bid for a pilot property |
| Hotel asset manager, former GC project manager | 5 | 6.5 | 7 | Ship it; would invite a bid on a small first project |

The reviewers ran on a lighter model than the one that built the site, to fit the account's usage limits. They worked from the rendered page text, screenshots and the source files.

## What each round changed

**Round 1: say exactly what Atmos did.**
- Every project card and page states Atmos's role. The multifamily work is site and landscape design, and other firms were the building designer and general contractor.
- The hotel page stopped implying past hotel work.
- Outcome claims with no data behind them ("units back online sooner") became process commitments (a day-by-day schedule with every bid).
- The property managers page gained a five-step occupied-unit protocol and vendor-onboarding detail.
- The bid form's first step was shortened and its wording widened to fit rental owners as well as apartment and hotel managers.
- The thank-you page now lists next steps.
- SEO: target phrases in titles and leads, links between audience and service pages, a headers file that works on both likely hosts.

**Round 2: tighten the licensing and hospitality wording.**
- Project cards show status (complete, under construction, design only).
- The landscape page names licensed landscape and pool contractors and the landscape-architect carve-out.
- Hotel answers on FF&E, PIP approvals and design services were narrowed to what Atmos does.
- The form hides hotel-only options for short-term rental owners.
- Each project page got its own link-preview image.

**Round 3: accuracy and proofreading.**
- A case study that described work still under construction in the past tense was corrected.
- "Designed and built" was changed where the portfolio shows design only.
- Repeated phrases and unsupported lines were removed.
- Links were added from the hotel and property managers pages to the remaining service pages.

## What the reviewers asked for that only the owner can supply

All four buyer-side reviewers said the same thing: the site is clear and credible, and what holds the score below 9 is proof of completed renovation work for their kind of property. The list is in [LAUNCH.md](LAUNCH.md#8-what-to-add-as-you-win-work).
