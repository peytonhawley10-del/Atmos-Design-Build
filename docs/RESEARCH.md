# Research behind the redesign

Compiled November 2026 from web research (sources linked). Statistics are the publishers' own figures; spot-check any number before quoting it in sales material.

## 1. How the target buyers buy

**Multifamily and property management**
- Decision makers: the property or community manager and maintenance supervisor (turns, small capex), the regional manager (approvals), and the asset manager or director of construction (value-add programs, judged on return).
- What they rank above price: responsiveness, communication and reliability. Preferred-vendor status goes to firms that are "reliable, licensed, and easy to work with."
- Common failures in occupied renovations: frustrated residents, missed timelines, sloppy subcontractors, weak communication ([Housing Finance](https://www.housingfinance.com/developments/5-secrets-of-occupied-affordable-housing-renovation)).
- Compliance comes before any work. Managers onboard vendors through Yardi VendorCafe/VendorShield, RealPage Vendor Credentialing or NetVendor.
  - Typical COI minimum: $1M per occurrence and $2M aggregate general liability.
  - Both the management company and the owner entity are named as additional insured.
  - The legal name must match exactly across the W-9, the COI and the portal.
- California hook: the first **SB 721** balcony (exterior elevated element) inspection deadline was Jan 1, 2026, so many owners now have repair scopes.

**Hotels**
- Decision makers: owner or owner's rep, asset manager, GM, brand, designer, purchasing agent.
- A PIP is "contractual, brand-scoped, and deadline-bound." Owners are phasing PIPs and renovating while occupied.
- ADA (CBC 11B): alterations trigger path-of-travel upgrades.

**Short-term rentals**
- Owners buy on ADR, revenue and listing photos, and blocked nights cost money, so speed sells.
- San Diego whole-home STR licenses are capped ([sandiego.gov/stro](https://www.sandiego.gov/stro)). Owners upgrade the licensed units they already have.

## 2. Buyer vocabulary used in the copy

- **Multifamily:** unit turn, make-ready, down unit, days vacant, classic vs. renovated unit, interior upgrade package, rent premium, value-add, capex, NTE, scope sheet, punch list, occupied renovation, amenity refresh, curb appeal.
- **Hotels:** PIP, brand standards, FF&E vs. OS&E, soft goods vs. case goods, keys, model room, ADA guestrooms, RevPAR, ADR.
- **STR:** turnover, refresh, blocked nights, listing photos.

## 3. Conversion tactics applied

- **Primary CTA "Request a Bid"** for property managers and asset managers, who buy through bids. **Secondary CTA "Schedule a Property Walk,"** which is low commitment and matches how scopes actually get set.
- **Trust bar on every page:** CSLB license number, insurance, interior + exterior + landscape, service area, response promise.
- **Vendor packet** (COI, W-9, license) offered on request. It removes a procurement step.
- **Two-step qualifying form.**
  - Step 1: contact, role, property type.
  - Step 2: units/keys, location, scope, occupancy, timeline, budget band.
  - A "small job" option routes to the sister handyman company.
- **Speed to lead:** firms that respond within an hour are about 7x more likely to qualify the lead ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)).
- **Case-study spec block:** property type, units, scope, services, status, then before/after photos.

## 4. Competitors and whitespace

Local:
- Urban Renovation Co. (Carlsbad, institutional multifamily)
- Level 3 Construction (Vista, hotels)
- Gafcon (multifamily program management)
- Pacific Western Painting (HOA exteriors)
- Sun Country Builders (affordable housing)
- Gothic Landscape and BrightView (commercial landscape)
- STR designers (design only)

National benchmarks: Digney York (hotels), Renu, Adivo, ASH Renovations.

**What nobody local owns, and Atmos can:**
1. **Design-led renovation for mid-size owners:** roughly 8–150 units, independent hotels, STR portfolios. Institutional firms chase 900-unit work. Designers don't build, and builders don't design.
2. **Interior + exterior + landscape under one contract at mid-scale.** Competitors are siloed by trade.
3. **Turnkey STR and boutique hospitality.** No San Diego firm found offers design, build and furnishing as one package.
4. **Small-team responsiveness,** with one point of contact.
5. **A sister handyman company** for work orders, so the whole portfolio has one call.

**Patterns borrowed:** a plain-HTML stats bar (not JS counters that index as zeros), sector tiles, a case-study spec block, before/after comparison, a persistent CTA high on the page, and a site walk before the bid.

## 6. California advertising rules the site follows

1. **License number in all advertising** (B&P §7030.5). The footer on every page shows "CA Lic. #1154511" with a link to the CSLB lookup.
2. **Never advertise "bonded."** CSLB considers it misleading, so the site says "licensed & insured" only.
3. **B license scope** (B&P §7057). A B contractor primes projects with two or more unrelated trades. The landscape and exterior pages are written as multi-trade renovation; landscape-only jobs need a C-27 or a C-27 subcontractor.
4. **No "architect" or "landscape architect" titles** without that license. "Design" and "landscape design" are fine.
5. **No misleading advertising.** Renderings are labeled as renderings, and portfolio credits state Atmos's actual role, e.g. "Site & landscape design."
6. **CalOPPA:** the privacy policy is linked wherever the form collects personal information.
